# Problem Statement

## Background
The aviation industry relies heavily on the continuous and safe flow of aircraft through terminal airspace and on the ground at busy airports. Managing this complex choreography requires constant vigilance, as the environment is highly dynamic and constrained by weather conditions, runway geometry, and strict separation requirements. 

## The Problem
Air traffic controllers must manually predict the future positions of aircraft within crowded terminal airspace while simultaneously monitoring runway and taxiway traffic. Attempting to mentally synthesize live telemetry to predict potential conflicts creates an exceptionally high cognitive workload.

## Who is Affected
Air traffic controllers operating in terminal radar approach control (TRACON) facilities and local airport control towers who are responsible for managing high volumes of airborne and ground traffic at busy airports.

## Why It Matters
The intense cognitive load required to mentally project trajectories increases the likelihood of human error. This directly elevates the risk of catastrophic safety incidents, specifically mid-air collisions in terminal airspace and runway incursions on the ground, potentially endangering hundreds of lives per incident.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short
Current operational workflows often rely on controllers manually projecting aircraft paths, or they utilize systems that provide generic proximity warnings. These warnings often lack context regarding weather-aware ground stopping-distance envelopes or specific runway geometry. Furthermore, they typically do not provide actionable resolution advisories, forcing controllers to rapidly calculate safe avoidance maneuvers under immense pressure.
